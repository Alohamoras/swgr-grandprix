Driver: TestAutopilot v1.4 (the game's Autopilot ability), racing as Nik Skandaro (landspeeder), v0.16.1.

## The owner's aim (2026-11-11)
"The AI tuning we are shooting for doesn't have to be perfect, but I want it to feel **consistent and rewarding**."
Owner's observations: at higher cc the autopilot crashes more, and opponents attack you more. Both may be fair,
realistic parts of a fast class rather than faults: count them, don't filter them out.

So we judge a setting by two things: the margin a clean drive gets (rewarding) and how much that margin moves from
race to race (consistent).

"Open" = no catch-up, no slowdown (both 1.0, all three assets), no forced errors, speed matching off.

What it says:
- **With the help switched off, the bots run their own pace, and it's quick.** At 150cc the best bot beats a clean
  autopilot on The Giants' Graveyard by 6-7 s and is level on the others. At 50cc the best bot is 1-5 s behind.
- So the game's default 0.3 slowdown was what kept the field behind you. "Less rubber band" here means a harder AI,
  not an easier one. The tuning question becomes "how much to slow the bots", with no catch-up needed.
- **Open loop looks consistent.** At The War Fields (dawn) the best bot did 104.8 and 105.5 with the band off,
  against 105.2-116.4 with it on.
- **The autopilot is the noisy part.** Its War Fields (dawn) times run 98.3-111.0: about 99 clean, about 110 after a
  crash. Race 2 was a DNF: it was knocked off 5 times (the Autopilot ability had to re-fire each time) and never
  finished. This matches the owner's experience: more crashes and more attacks at 150cc.
- Pressure (the tier window) did little with the band off (two races, 95.2 and 108.9: too noisy to say).

## The autopilot against the owner
The War Fields (dawn) 150cc: the owner's record 1:42.39; the autopilot clean 1:38.3-1:38.8 (v0.16), 1:43.35
earlier (old AI). A clean autopilot run is about 3-4 s faster than the owner's best there. So a field that loses to
a clean autopilot by 3-4 s is level with the owner at their best.

| Setting | Track | Autopilot | Best AI | 3rd AI | Your place |

| 100 open | The Giants' Graveyard | 118.6, 116.7 | 115.8, 115.6 | 118.3, 116.7 | 4, 3 |

| 150 open | The War Fields (dawn) | 102.7, 108.2 | 105.3, 104.3 | 109.0, 108.2 | 1, 3 |
| 150 calm (aggression vs you: chance x0.5, cooldown x2) | The War Fields (dawn) | 98.8, 105.4, 105.4 | 108.4, 99.5, 99.4 | 110.5, 105.4, 103.9 | 1, 3, 4 |
| 150 calm | The Giants' Graveyard | 100.0, 101.9 | 94.3, 94.7 | 103.0, 101.9 | 2, 3 |
| 150 gentle (slowdown 0.85) | The War Fields (dawn) | 106.8, 102.3, DNF | 102.9, 107.3, - | 117.3, 112.6, - | 2, 1, DNF |
| 150 gentle | The Giants' Graveyard | 97.4, 96.7 | 99.1, 95.1 | 101.6, 97.3 | 1, 2 |
| 200 open | The War Fields (dawn) | 98.0, 104.3 | 108.5, 95.1 | 108.6, 99.4 | 1, 5 |

What it adds:
- **Open loop is the consistent setting.** The best bot at The War Fields (dawn) 150cc, four open runs over two
  batches: 104.8, 105.5, 105.3, 104.3 s. A one-second band. With the game's band on it was 105.2-116.4.
- **The slowdown only acts when a bot is ahead of you** (it is "ahead of its window"). With you leading, only
  catch-up acts, and at 1.0 there is none. So slowdown is the mercy rule (it lets you catch a leader back) and
  catch-up is the punishment rule (it reels you in when you lead). For "skill shows", catch-up is the one to drop.
  Gentle 0.85 didn't measurably slow the leaders in 5 races.
- **Calmer aggression made the leaders faster** (War Fields 99.4-99.5 in two of three), presumably less time spent
  attacking. No DNF in 5 calm races, but 2 in 36 races overall is too rare to call. Leave aggression to the owner's
  feel, as they suggested.
- **100cc open: the bots are level with a clean autopilot** (within 1-3 s either way); 200cc open looks like 150cc.
- **The Giants' Graveyard favours the bots** at every class (sprint, long straights): 150cc 94-95 s against the
  autopilot's 97-102.
- The two DNFs (2 in 36) were both The War Fields (dawn) 150cc with the autopilot knocked off several times.

## Proposal (not applied; the owner's races decide)
The direction, by the owner's aim ("consistent and rewarding"):
1. **Catch-up off at every class** (MaxAiCatchupScale 1.0 on all three assets): when you lead, the lead is yours.
   This is what makes a clean drive pay.
2. **Speed matching off, forced errors off from 100cc** (50cc keeps the game's errors as a beginner's gift).
3. **The mercy rule shrinks with the class** (MaxAiSlowdownScale when a bot leads you): 50cc 0.5, 100cc 0.7,
   150cc 0.85, 200cc 0.85. The game default is 0.3 (heavy).
4. Aggression: the game's own at 50-100cc; at 150cc the owner says whether the knock-offs feel fair before we touch it.
5. Then the owner races 100cc and 150cc (one cup each) and we read their `[gaps]` lines: the target is winning by a
   few seconds on a good race and losing on a messy one, with the best bot's time steady from race to race.

Open questions: whether the mercy rule actually slows a leading bot (needs a run where the autopilot is held back,
e.g. a race started late); the tier windows (`PacingConfigs`, 11 entries) can't be read from Lua, so pressure can
only be judged by results.
